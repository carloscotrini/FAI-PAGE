The loop you kept running
  • Guess a value for ŵ.

The only step you improvised was the last one. Let's pin it down.

Which way, and how far?

Two ways to turn the shout into a correction. You probably used the second one without noticing.

Fixed step: the first instinct

if l < 0: ŵ ← ŵ − α
else:    ŵ ← ŵ + α

Only the sign of the miss is used. Every step is the same size: painfully slow when you are far off, jittery once you are close.

Proportional step: what you actually did

ŵ ← ŵ + α · l

Use the size of the miss too. Shouted "too low by 116,889"? Big correction. "Too high by 111"? A nudge. One rule handles both.

The fixed-step algorithm.

Here is the whole thing, written as a program.

given   x, y
guess   ŵ
repeat max_rounds times
    ŷ = ŵ · x
    l = y − ŷ
    ŵ ← ŵ + α · l

  • ŵ: your guess for the price per m².
  • l: the signed miss. Positive means raise the price.
  • α: the step size. You choose it.
  • max_rounds: how many times the loop runs.

Reading the shout.

The market shouts l = +8,789 for Rosenweg 12. What does that tell you?

l = y − ŷ > 0: the house sold for 8,789 more than you said. ŵ ← ŵ + α · l then raises ŵ. The sign of the miss is the direction of the correction.

Choosing the step size.

You pick a big step size, α = 0.05, for Rosenweg 12 (x = 234). What happens round after round?

One update moves the prediction by α · l · x = 11.7 · l: past the target and further away on the other side. Whenever α · x > 2 the loop diverges, which is why α is tiny here, around 0.001 to 0.004.

Two knobs, one miss.

Two runs of fit_street start from w = 0, b = 0, and both stop with the average miss below ε. One convinces all eight owners, the other only five. What made the difference?

Every round moves w and b in the same proportion, αw to αb. Any line through the average house silences the average miss, so that proportion decides which line the loop stops on: too steep, too flat, or one that convinces the whole street.
